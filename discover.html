Explore the universe

Planets

Worlds orbiting stars. Our solar system has eight recognized planets, from rocky Mercury to ice giant Neptune.

Key fact: Jupiter's mass is more than twice that of all the other planets combined.

Galaxies

Vast gravitational systems containing stars, gas, dust, stellar remnants, and dark matter.

Key fact: The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy; our Sun lies in one of its spiral arms.

Black Holes

Regions whose event horizons mark the boundary beyond which escape is impossible.

Key fact: The first image of a black hole's shadow (M87*) was released in 2019 by the Event Horizon Telescope.

Nebulae

Interstellar clouds of gas and dust. Some are stellar nurseries; others are remnants of dying stars.

Key fact: The Orion Nebula is visible to the naked eye as the fuzzy middle "star" of Orion's sword.

Exoplanets

Planets beyond our solar system, detected using methods such as transits and radial velocity.

Key fact: The first exoplanet found around a Sun-like star, 51 Pegasi b, was announced in 1995.

Neutron Stars

Ultra-dense stellar remnants. Some rotate rapidly and emit beams observed as pulsars.

Key fact: A teaspoon of neutron-star material would weigh billions of tonnes.
