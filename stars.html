Stellar evolution

The Life of a Star

Every star is a long-running balance between gravity and the energy released by nuclear fusion.

How stars work

Stars form when dense regions of molecular clouds collapse under gravity. As the core heats and compresses, hydrogen fusion can begin. The star then spends much of its life on the main sequence.

    Stage 3 of 6

    Main sequence

    Hydrogen fusion in the core supplies energy that counteracts gravitational collapse.

    Stages at a glance

    Stage 1

    Nebula

    Cold gas and dust gather into dense regions that may collapse to form stars.

    Stage 2

    Protostar

    A forming star heats as gravity compresses its material. Fusion has not yet stabilized.

    Stage 3

    Main Sequence

    Hydrogen fusion in the core supplies energy that counteracts gravitational collapse.

    Stage 4

    Giant phase

    After core hydrogen is depleted, a star's structure changes. The details depend on its mass.

    Possible ending

    White Dwarf

    Many low- and intermediate-mass stars leave behind a hot, dense stellar core.

    Possible ending

    Supernova remnant

    Massive stars may explode as supernovae, leaving neutron stars or black holes.

    Fast facts about stars

    • Star colour is related to surface temperature: blue stars are generally hotter than red stars.
    • The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old.
    • A star's mass is a major factor controlling its lifetime and final fate.
    • The elements in planets and living organisms were forged through earlier generations of stars and stellar explosions.
